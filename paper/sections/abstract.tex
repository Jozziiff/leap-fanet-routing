\begin{abstract}
Routing in flying ad hoc networks suffers from links that break as UAVs move, and most protocols react only
after a transmission has failed. We first measure where packets are lost. On the UavNetSim-v1 simulator, whose
deterministic physical layer lets us classify every failed reception as out of range or interference, we
attribute the losses of seven protocols (DSDV, Greedy, GRAd, OPAR, Q-Routing, QGeo, Q-FANET) over 231 runs.
Collisions almost never cause a loss, since MAC retransmissions absorb them, whereas link breaks cost Greedy
forwarding 9.8\,\% of its packets at 10\,m/s and 16.0\,\% at 20\,m/s. We then propose LEAP-GR, a distributed
geographic protocol that forwards like Greedy unless the chosen neighbour's link expiration time, computed
from positions and velocities carried in hello messages, falls below a threshold. With an identical MAC and
paired seeds, LEAP-GR raises the packet delivery ratio over Greedy in 9 of 11 configurations, by up to 14.7
points (98.7\,\% against 84.0\,\% at 20\,m/s), at the cost of slightly longer paths and 128 bits per hello. An
ablation with a reactive control shows that prediction beats switching after a failure by 0.9 to 3.0 points
at high speed and density, but not at low speed or under saturation. Most of the gain comes from discarding
neighbours that have already left, which the platform's 2\,s neighbour lifetime keeps in the table. A
cross-layer weighting by MAC retransmission rate does not improve delivery.
\end{abstract}
